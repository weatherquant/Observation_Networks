# A Decision-Support Framework for Cost-Constrained Surface Observation Networks

This study develops a decision-support framework for evaluating how surface-observation networks can preserve forecast value and service exposure under constrained maintenance budgets. 

Observation-impact diagnostics, spatial population exposure and a remoteness-based cost index are combined within a budget-constrained portfolio framework, supported by regression and spatial analyses. 

Across an Australian case study of 785 SYNOP stations, substantial variation is observed in forecast impact, population exposure and maintenance burden. 

Portfolio results indicate that a disproportionate share of beneficial forecast impact can be retained as available expenditure declines. Under the baseline specification, an 80% cost-index budget retained 93.5% of beneficial forecast impact and 99.5% of aggregate population-exposure score. 

Observation availability was positively associated with forecast impact, while the effects of remoteness and population exposure depended on their joint spatial structure, indicating that remoteness alone provides an inadequate basis for network rationalisation. 

The results further show that forecast impact and maintenance burden are only weakly aligned, creating opportunities to preserve substantial forecast value while reducing expenditure. 

The framework provides a transparent and reproducible approach for identifying robust retention and review priorities and is potentially transferable to other observing systems that require explicit trade-offs between information value, service exposure and operating cost. 

The analysis is intended for strategic screening rather than operational network redesign because it relies on proxy costs, deterministic inputs and heuristic portfolio selection and has not been validated through independent observing-system experiments. 

Its principal implication is that observation-network investment decisions require explicit consideration of forecast value, service exposure, operational cost and uncertainty.
